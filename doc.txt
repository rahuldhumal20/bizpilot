# BizPilot – AI Business Consultant
## Software Requirements Specification (SRS) and Project Documentation

**Document Version:** 1.1  
**Project Type:** AI-Powered Business Analysis and Recommendation System  
**Development Stage:** MVP Implementation and Finalization  
**Architecture:** React + FastAPI + LangGraph  
**LLM Provider:** Groq

---

# 1. Executive Summary

BizPilot is an AI-powered business consulting application designed to help small businesses and startups understand operational challenges and identify practical solutions.

Users provide information about their business, describe their problems, and specify their available budget. BizPilot processes the information through five specialized AI agents coordinated using LangGraph.

A supporting Python-based financial validation component checks the financial estimates before the Project Planner generates an implementation roadmap.

The application produces a structured Business Intelligence Report containing:

1. Business Analysis
2. Solution Architecture
3. Cost Analysis
4. Financial Validation
5. Project Roadmap
6. Critic Review

The primary objective is to demonstrate how Generative AI and agent orchestration can support practical business problem-solving.

# 2. Problem Statement

Small businesses frequently experience operational difficulties such as:

- Manual record-keeping and repetitive administrative tasks.
- Inefficient inventory, order, and customer management.
- Lack of suitable digital tools.
- Difficulty selecting appropriate technology solutions.
- Uncertainty about implementation costs.
- Limited technical knowledge.
- Difficulty planning technology adoption.

Professional consulting services may be expensive or inaccessible to smaller businesses.

BizPilot addresses this problem by providing an AI-assisted initial business assessment and structured recommendations through a web application.

# 3. Project Objectives

The main objectives are:

1. Understand business problems using AI.
2. Identify operational challenges and their likely root causes.
3. Recommend practical solutions based on business requirements.
4. Estimate initial and recurring implementation costs.
5. Validate financial estimates using Python.
6. Generate a structured implementation roadmap.
7. Review the proposal for quality, feasibility, and risks.
8. Present the results through a user-friendly dashboard.

# 4. Target Users

- Small business owners.
- Startup founders.
- Entrepreneurs.
- Local service providers.
- Business operators interested in digital transformation.

# 5. Functional Requirements

## 5.1 Business Information Input

The application accepts the following information:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Business Name | Name of the business |
| Industry | Business industry |
| Industry Domain | Specific business category |
| Business Description | Overview of existing operations |
| Business Problem | Main challenge requiring a solution |
| Budget Amount | Available implementation budget |
| Currency | INR, USD, EUR or GBP |
| Budget Scope | Initial-only or first-year total |
| Business Size | Startup, Small Business, Medium Business or Enterprise |

Supported industry domains include retail, beauty, food, healthcare, education, manufacturing, water, agriculture, hospitality, logistics, professional services, and other.

## 5.2 Business Analysis

The Business Analyst identifies:

- Business overview.
- Operational problems.
- Root causes.
- Business impact.
- Customer experience issues.
- Automation opportunities.
- Assumptions.
- Missing information.

The analysis must distinguish user-provided facts from assumptions and avoid inventing business information.

## 5.3 Solution Architecture

The Solution Architect designs a practical solution based on:

- Business requirements.
- Identified problems.
- Industry.
- Business size.
- Available budget.
- Operational constraints.

The output includes:

- Requirement understanding.
- Recommended solution.
- Required resources.
- Alternative solutions.
- Budget considerations.
- Implementation requirements.
- Risks and limitations.
- Missing information.

Vendor prices and product capabilities must be treated as unverified unless supported by reliable information.

## 5.4 Cost Analysis

The Cost Analyst estimates:

- Initial implementation cost.
- Monthly operating expenses.
- Annual gross benefits, when sufficient information exists.
- Financial assumptions.
- Financial risks.

Costs should be expressed as reasonable ranges where appropriate.

Unknown benefits must remain unknown. The system must not invent revenue, savings, or ROI figures.

## 5.5 Financial Validation

A Python-based financial validation component checks the structured financial estimates.

Its responsibilities include:

- Checking required financial fields.
- Validating numeric values.
- Checking minimum and maximum ranges.
- Verifying currency consistency.
- Comparing estimated costs with the available budget.
- Handling initial-only and first-year-total budget scopes.
- Calculating financial indicators only when sufficient information is available.

Possible statuses include:

- Passed
- Needs Review
- Failed
- Not Validated

The financial validator is a supporting Python component, not a sixth AI agent.

## 5.6 Project Planning

The Project Planner creates an implementation roadmap with three phases:

**Phase 1 – MVP / Initial Setup**
- Foundational setup.
- Required resources.
- Initial configuration.
- Dependencies.
- Deliverables.

**Phase 2 – Core Implementation**
- Main implementation tasks.
- Integration and configuration.
- Operational workflow.
- Dependencies.
- Deliverables.

**Phase 3 – Testing and Rollout**
- Functional testing.
- User acceptance testing.
- Training.
- Deployment or rollout.
- Handover and monitoring.

The roadmap also includes key dependencies, testing strategy, success metrics, risks, and information required before implementation.

The roadmap must remain consistent with the proposed solution and financial estimates.

## 5.7 Critic Review

The Critic Agent reviews the complete proposal across five dimensions:

1. Problem Understanding
2. Solution Suitability
3. Financial Feasibility
4. Implementation Feasibility
5. Risk and Compliance Management

The output includes:

- Quality score.
- Category scores.
- Approval recommendation.
- Business strengths.
- Financial concerns.
- Technical concerns.
- Missing information.
- Critical risks.

Possible recommendations include Approved, Conditional Approval, Rejected, and Manual Review Required.

The Critic's assessment is an AI-generated review, not a guarantee of actual business feasibility.

## 5.8 Final Business Intelligence Report

The backend combines the agent outputs into one report.

The report contains:

1. Business information.
2. Business Analysis.
3. Solution Architecture.
4. Cost Analysis.
5. Financial Validation.
6. Project Roadmap.
7. Critic Review.
8. Overall assessment.

## 5.9 Frontend Dashboard

The React application provides:

- Business information form.
- Industry and business-size selection.
- Budget and currency inputs.
- Budget-scope selection.
- Analysis submission.
- Agent execution status.
- Structured report display.
- Markdown formatting.

The dashboard displays five specialist AI agents. The financial validator is not counted as a sixth agent.

# 7. System Workflow

BizPilot follows a sequential LangGraph workflow.

1. User submits business information.
2. FastAPI validates the request.
3. Industry context is loaded.
4. Business Analyst performs the analysis.
5. Solution Architect designs the solution.
6. Cost Analyst estimates financial requirements.
7. Financial Validator checks the financial data.
8. Project Planner creates the implementation roadmap.
9. Critic Agent reviews the complete proposal.
10. Final report is compiled.
11. FastAPI returns the result.
12. React displays the report.

**Workflow:**

User Input → FastAPI → Business Analyst → Solution Architect → Cost Analyst → Financial Validator → Project Planner → Critic Agent → Final Report → Dashboard

LangGraph manages workflow execution and the passing of shared state between nodes.

# 9. System Architecture

The application uses a frontend-backend architecture with a separate AI processing workflow.

## 9.1 Frontend Layer

Responsibilities:

- Collect business information.
- Submit requests to the backend.
- Display analysis status.
- Present the final report.
- Display agent execution logs.

## 9.2 Backend Layer

Responsibilities:

- Expose REST API endpoints.
- Validate incoming requests.
- Load relevant industry context.
- Execute the LangGraph workflow.
- Compile the final report.
- Return results to the frontend.

## 9.3 AI Processing Layer

Responsibilities:

- Execute the five specialist AI agents.
- Generate structured business recommendations.
- Process agent outputs using shared workflow state.
- Produce the final proposal.

## 9.4 Financial Validation Layer

Responsibilities:

- Validate financial fields.
- Check budget feasibility.
- Identify inconsistencies.
- Calculate supported financial indicators.

# 10. API Requirements

## Business Analysis Endpoint

**Method:** POST  
**Endpoint:** `/api/analyze`

### Request Fields

| Field | Type |
|---|---|
| business_name | String |
| industry | String |
| industry_domain | String |
| business_description | String |
| business_problem | String |
| budget_amount | Number |
| currency | String |
| budget_scope | String |
| business_size | String |

### Response

The endpoint returns:

- Success status.
- Response message.
- Final report.
- Agent execution logs.

### Error Handling

The application should provide understandable errors when:

- Request validation fails.
- Required information is missing.
- An agent fails.
- LLM inference fails.
- The workflow cannot produce a complete report.

Errors must not expose API keys or sensitive configuration.

# 11. Non-Functional Requirements

## Performance

- Keep LLM prompts concise.
- Avoid unnecessary workflow execution.
- Handle model output limits.
- Provide clear execution status.

## Reliability

- Handle missing financial information safely.
- Avoid fabricated financial calculations.
- Identify incomplete or failed agent outputs.
- Avoid presenting incomplete reports as successful results.

## Security

- Keep Groq API keys in backend environment variables.
- Exclude `.env` files from Git.
- Never expose API credentials in frontend code.
- Do not return sensitive configuration in API responses.

## Usability

- Provide a clear business information form.
- Display readable validation messages.
- Organize reports into meaningful sections.
- Clearly explain assumptions and limitations.

## Maintainability

- Keep agent modules separate.
- Maintain the existing API, workflow, domain, and validator structure.
- Avoid unnecessary architectural complexity.

## 14.1 Known Limitations

Testing has identified areas that still need attention:

1. Agent responses may be incomplete when model output limits are reached.
2. Business Analysis and Solution Architecture can make unverified vendor or product claims.
3. Cost estimates can differ between agents.
4. Annual financial benefits may be unavailable without business data.
5. The Critic can identify inconsistencies but does not automatically correct them.
6. Vendor pricing, product features, and regulatory requirements require independent verification.
7. Production deployment and live environment testing have not yet been confirmed.

These are finalization issues, not reasons to expand the architecture.

# 18. Final Project Definition

BizPilot is a LangGraph-based AI business consulting application that uses five specialized AI agents to analyze business challenges, recommend practical solutions, estimate implementation costs, generate project roadmaps, and review proposal quality.

A Python-based financial validation component checks financial estimates before the final business intelligence report is generated.

The application demonstrates practical use of:

- Generative AI.
- Multi-agent orchestration.
- LangChain and LangGraph.
- Python and FastAPI.
- React frontend development.
- Structured financial validation.
- Business problem analysis.
- End-to-end application integration.

**Final Development Principle**

Complete the agreed MVP first. Fix existing functionality when necessary. Do not introduce new features or unnecessary architectural complexity before the application works reliably from user input to final report.
